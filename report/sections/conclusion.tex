\section{Conclusion}

This project investigated whether room occupancy can be inferred from a single
$\text{CO}_2$ sensor by modelling the observed series with a Hidden Markov
Model. Five nested 4-state Gaussian HMMs were fitted, organised along the
hierarchy of Figure~\ref{fig:diagram:model_hierarchy}: a baseline HMM(1), an
AR(1) and AR(2) emission extension, a second-order latent chain with AR(1)
emissions, and the fully combined AR(2), HMM(2) model. All models were
implemented and fitted using \texttt{JAX}, \texttt{EQUINOX} and \texttt{Optax}.

The likelihood-ratio tests in
Table~\ref{tab:lrt_comparison} reject every base model in favour of its
expanded counterpart at $p < 10^{-4}$, confirming that both adding
autoregressive structure to the emissions and relaxing the first-order Markov
assumption produce statistically significant gains in fit. The single largest
improvement is the introduction of an AR(1) emission on top of the ordinary
HMM, with a likelihood-ratio statistic of $\Lambda \approx 2058$ on four
degrees of freedom and a drop in AIC of more than two thousand units. This
indicates that most of the temporal structure left unexplained by the
baseline HMM is autoregressive in nature, not a deficiency of the
first-order latent chain.

AIC and BIC, however, disagree on the recommended model.
Table~\ref{tab:model_stats} shows that AIC decreases monotonically along the
hierarchy and is minimised by AR(2), HMM(2), reflecting the genuine gain in
log-likelihood from the largest model. BIC, which penalises additional
parameters more heavily, instead selects AR(2), HMM(1): both second-order
models add many parameters (one variance and AR coefficient per ordered
state pair) without enough likelihood improvement to offset the penalty on
this sample size. The takeaway is that the second-order chain pays for
itself in fit but not in parsimony.

For practical use as an occupancy model, where interpretability and a
manageable number of per-state parameters are valuable, AR(2), HMM(1) is the
recommended model: it captures the within-regime autocorrelation that the
plain HMM cannot, while keeping the latent dynamics first-order and the
number of free parameters modest. AR(2), HMM(2) remains the strict best-fit
model and is the appropriate choice when raw likelihood is the only
criterion. Possible extensions to either model --- covariate-dependent
transitions, richer emission families, or further parameter tying in the
second-order chain --- are discussed in the Future Work section.
